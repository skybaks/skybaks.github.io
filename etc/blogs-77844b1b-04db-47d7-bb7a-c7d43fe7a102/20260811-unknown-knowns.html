The Unknown Knowns

2026-08-11

Donald Rumsfeld, the United States Secretary of Defense under president George W. Bush, had a somewhat infamous quote during a news briefing on February 12, 2002:

Q: Could I follow up, Mr. Secretary, on what you just said, please? In regard to Iraq weapons of mass destruction and terrorists, is there any evidence to indicate that Iraq has attempted to or is willing to supply terrorists with weapons of mass destruction? Because there are reports that there is no evidence of a direct link between Baghdad and some of these terrorist organizations.

Rumsfeld: Reports that say that something hasn't happened are always interesting to me, because as we know, there are known knowns; there are things we know we know. We also know there are known unknowns; that is to say we know there are some things we do not know. But there are also unknown unknowns -- the ones we don't know we don't know. And if one looks throughout the history of our country and other free countries, it is the latter category that tend to be the difficult ones.

And so people who have the omniscience that they can say with high certainty that something has not happened or is not being tried, have capabilities that are -- what was the word you used, Pam, earlier?

Q: Free associate? (laughs)

Rumsfeld: Yeah. They can -- (chuckles) -- they can do things I can't do. (laughter)

Q: Excuse me. But is this an unknown unknown?

Rumsfeld: I'm not --

Q: Because you said several unknowns, and I'm just wondering if this is an unknown unknown.

Rumsfeld: I'm not going to say which it is.

The source of the above quote courtesy of Wikipedia: https://web.archive.org/web/20160406235718/http://archive.defense.gov/Transcripts/Transcript.aspx?TranscriptID=2636

So with the added context, we can see that during the press conference the reporter asked a hard question about the weapons of mass destruction and Donald needed to employ some fancy footwork to give them a politician non-answer. The beauty in this particular non-answer is that it sounds like complete gibberish at first but when you think of it logically it makes complete sense. However, aside from the fun wordplay, did Donald Rumsfeld answer the serious question regarding weapons of mass destruction? Absolutely he did not! 🤣

The table above shows each of the 4 combinations of the phrase. From the briefing we can see that Donald Rumsfeld lists and explains only 3 of the 4. The known knowns he explains by saying that these are things we know we know. The known unknowns he explains by saying that there are things we know that we don't know. Finally, he goes over the unknown unknowns as things that we don't know, and we don't even realize it. All this is well and good, but there is one more combination to complete the square:

The Unknown Knowns

Since Donald does not raise or explain the idea of the unknown known does that mean that it possibly doesn't exist? To follow on with the pattern of explanation that was given for the other 3, this might have been explained as (not a real Donald Rumsfeld quotation):

There is also the unknown knowns; or rather the things we don't realize that we know.

The easiest example I can think of to explain this might be a communication breakdown between two individuals or groups of people. The knowldge of one person is real but it is not always shared or brought out to the group. If the other person were responsible for making a hard decision then they could possibly be only asking specific questions of the other person and, through no fault of either person, neglect to perform exactly the right interrogation which would tease out that knowledge, thus resulting in a less informed decision.

This disconnect becomes quite pervasive when working within a large group of people who do not all have an intimate shared understanding. If the group leaders don't know how to ask the right questions then they won't get the right answers. However, the onus is also partially on the knowledge holders to know when the right time is to interject even when not directly addressed.

The existence of unknown knowns is indicative of a problem in communication, I think. In a perfect world we should be able to turn all these unknown knowns into known knowns, and then throw our remaining effort into tackling the known unknowns.
